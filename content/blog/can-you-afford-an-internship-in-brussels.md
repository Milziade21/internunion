An internship offer in Brussels is exciting until you do the arithmetic. So let's do it honestly.

## The numbers

A single room in a shared Brussels flat runs about **€509/month** on the wider market. Add the essentials — utilities, groceries, and transport — and the minimum monthly cost of simply existing here lands near **€770** for someone under 25 who qualifies for the €1/month transport pass.

Against that, the [directory on this site](/city.html) shows stipends ranging widely. The median is around **€1,400/month**, which is livable if tight. But the low end is where the problem concentrates: several well-known organisations pay **€950–€1,100**, which leaves only **€180–€325** after the minimum floor — for people doing real, often sensitive, work.

## Why the low end matters most

This is not really about the median. It is about who gets filtered out. When a placement pays below the cost of living, only people whose families can subsidise them can take it. That quietly decides who gets to start a career in the institutions built on the promise of democracy.

## What you can do

- Check the [map](/city.html) before you apply — filter by "paid" to see where a stipend is actually offered.
- Know [your rights](/rights.html): if you have graduated, you may be entitled to the CIP minimum.
- [Add your own numbers](/submit.html). The more real stipends we collect, the harder underpayment is to hide.

The goal is not to discourage anyone from coming to Brussels. It is to make sure that the choice is not secretly reserved for the wealthy.
