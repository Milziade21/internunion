---
title: Is my unpaid internship in Brussels legal?
date: 2026-07-09
summary: The short answer: it depends on whether you are still a student. Here is how to tell if your placement should be paid.
---

The most common question interns arriving in Brussels ask is also the hardest to answer simply: **is it legal for this to be unpaid?** Belgium has no single "intern" status, so the honest answer is *it depends on which contract you are on*.

## If you are still enrolled at a university

An **academic internship** (convention de stage / stageovereenkomst) is a three-way agreement between you, your university and the employer, tied to your studies. It is generally **legal to be unpaid** — the law only requires reimbursement of real, documented expenses. The trade-off is that it is supposed to be a *learning experience*, with a training plan, not a job where you quietly replace a staff member.

## If you have finished your studies

Then things change. As a graduate or jobseeker you should normally be on a **Professional Immersion Agreement (CIP)** or an employment contract. The CIP carries a **legally indexed minimum stipend — about €1,095 gross per month for a full-time placement in 2026** — and requires a written training plan overseen by Bruxelles Formation or VDAB.

## The "bogus internship" test

Some employers keep recent graduates on a student-style, unpaid agreement to dodge the CIP minimum. If you have graduated and you are doing **real, productive work**, that is the warning sign. The European Committee of Social Rights has already found that Belgium's legal gaps here fail to protect interns from being used as cheap or free labour.

A rough test: *if the placement looks like a job, it should probably be paid like one.*

## What to do

- Ask, in writing, **which contract** you are being offered and whether it includes the CIP stipend.
- If you have finished studying and are being offered an unpaid "academic" convention, that is worth questioning.
- Get free advice from a **trade union** (CSC/ACV, FGTB/ABVV, CGSLB/ACLVB) or from **Bruxelles Formation**.

See the [Know your rights](/rights.html) page for the full picture, and if you have interned here, [add your conditions](/submit.html) so the next person knows what to expect.

*This is general information, not legal advice.*
